### 1.3 Why "226 checks → 245 reasons" cannot be reproduced, and the fix

On the tape (all three P13 families, 21:51 PT 10-27 → 07:05 PT 10-28) the legacy counters total **1,618 reason increments over 1,621 checks** — the runner's `nofill[...]["reasons"]` already increments **at most once per snapshot** (the 3 missing are STALE re-checks). Reasons can only exceed checks if "checks" is counted from a filtered tape (e.g. rows with `p_cal` present, or a per-close dedupe) while the reasons are summed over every poll. The real defects were different: (i) the runner's order (d_small → ts_timing → fav/size → edge) is its control flow, not a diagnostic order; (ii) `fav_gt_95_or_size` lumps three states — **no offer at all** (NO side has no bids → `yes_ask` encoded as 1.00/size 0), a real offer above the cap, and an offer thinner than the lot.

Implemented: `src/predhft/reject_taxonomy.py::classify` — ONE primary reason per check in the fixed order **data_defect > feed_health > timing_exclusion > risk_constraint > no_economic_opportunity**, plus the full list of failing flags. The runner now writes `reject`, `reject_flags`, `edge_c` on every P13 snapshot and a `first={...}` dict in the NOFILL line (legacy `reasons` kept for continuity).

Vocabulary used throughout: **check** = one poll of one market inside the trading window; **unique market** = one 15-min close of one series; **side-eligible** = passed d/window/cap/depth (the runner's re-check stage); **candidate** = passed everything incl. edge, gate and sizing (a paper order would be booked); **submission** = in paper there is no order object — the paper fill *is* the submission; **partial fill** = none can occur (paper fills the full lot or nothing); **completed fill** = `PAPER BUY`.

## 2. P13 feasibility and price handling

### 2.1 Joint feasibility by snapshot (official-feed era 16:00 PT 10-27 → 08:05 PT 10-28; 2,853 checks, 192 markets)

| asset | checks | not T | T, not D | T∧D, not F | T∧D∧F, not E | **all four** | closes with ≥1 feasible snapshot | … ignoring the arm window |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|

| ETH | 883 | 327 | 127 | 420 | 9 | **0** | 0 | 1 |
| SOL | 986 | 416 | 227 | 341 | 2 | **0** | 0 | 1 |

Reading: when the projection is informative (|d| ≥ 1 bp) the favourite is almost always already pinned (no NO bids) or offered above .95 (T∧D,¬F = 1,175 of 1,221 informative in-window snapshots, 96%). In the 46 snapshots where a ≤.95 offer with depth existed inside the windows, p_cal's edge over the price was below 5c in 45 — the market was priced where the model is. The single all-four snapshot (BTC) was a `d_small` on the very next snapshot (died in execution). **The rule as specified has essentially no feasible set on this tape**: the drought is not a bug in the checks, it is the market pricing the same information ~5–55 s earlier than our window allows us to act.

### 2.2 Expected net profit per contract (no double counting)

`E[net] = P(side pays) − E[exec price] − fee`, where exec price = executable offer for 10 contracts (top-3 depth where recorded; top level otherwise) **plus** the 1-tick slippage the paper runner books (0.01 — ten 0.001 ticks, so the paper price is *conservative* relative to a marketable order at the touch), and fee = `kalshi_taker_fee(10, price)/10` once. Latency is handled by the survival test (§5), not by a second spread/slippage term. Paired results in §4 give the mean of this quantity over every offered market at each horizon: **negative at every horizon for the official-feed forecast** (−9.3c at T-60 → −1.8c at T-10, pooled). Sensitivity: booking fills at the touch instead of touch+0.01 would add 783 × $0.01 = **+$7.83** to the 97-trade book (−$11.29 → −$3.46); this is a paper-pricing convention, not evidence of edge.

### 2.3 Price handling verified

- **YES/NO conversion**: Kalshi returns bids only (`orderbook_fp.yes_dollars`, `no_dollars`); YES ask = 1 − best NO bid; NO ask = 1 − best YES bid. Correct in `book_touch`; NO fills priced at `1 − yes_bid + 0.01`.
- **Units / ticks**: dollars with **sub-penny 0.001 ticks on 15M markets** (72% of recorded quotes are off the 1c grid, e.g. 0.042/0.043) and **fractional sizes** (`count_fp`, e.g. 416,689.19). All comparisons (cap .95, lot 10) are unit-correct; `ts_levers.TICK = 0.01` and the runner's +0.01 are 10 real ticks — conservative, documented above.
- **Missing-book fallback (the lead)**: the legacy encoding `yes_ask = 1.00, ask_size = 0` meant "no NO bids" and was indistinguishable from a real 1.00 offer. On the overnight window **323 of 1,621 in-window rows** carried it; in every one the YES bid was 0.999 (pinned) — i.e. a real absence of a seller, **not a data defect** — but the log's `min_fav=1.000` reads as "priced at 1.00". No row with a real 1.00 offer exists (a NO bid at 0.001 gives yes_ask 0.999). Fix: `s7_live.book_sides()` returns `None` for an absent side plus top-3 depth; the snapshot now writes `yes_ask: null` / `yes_bid: null`, `yes_ask_present`, `yes_bid_present`, `bids`, `asks`; `ts_levers.normalise`, `bet_gate._quotes` and `reject_taxonomy.side_present` treat `None` as "no offer" (legacy sentinels still accepted). The rule arithmetic in `run()` was already safe (`0 < ya ≤ cap` fails on the sentinel); only the representation and the counters were wrong.
- **Quote freshness**: cfb tick → local receipt p50 **0.068 s** (p90 0.093, max 1.09 s, n=33,240 per asset); receipt → book snapshot (`cfb_age_s`) p50 **0.51 s**, p90 0.90, max 1.74 s; book poll gap inside the final minute p50 1.1–1.9 s (BTC/SOL) but **p50 5.0 s for ETH** (ETH is pinned more often and pinned markets are polled every 5 s) — the ETH window is sampled ~11 times per close.

## 3. Settlement model vs the contract

Contract (rules_primary, every traded 15M series): "If the simple average of the sixty seconds of CF Benchmarks' <BRTI|ETHUSDRTI|SOLUSDRTI> before <close> is **at least** the simple average of the sixty seconds … before <open>, then Yes." `strike_type = greater_or_equal`; `floor_strike` = the open-window average (BTC/ETH 2 dp, SOL 4 dp).

Checked against the feed (`data/cfb/*.jsonl`, 66 closes per asset in the official era):

| alignment tested | BTC max |err| bp | ETH | SOL |
|---|---|---|---|
| **[T-60 s, T) value_ts, our `cfb_feed._partial` window** | **0.0006** (65/66 exact at 2 dp) | **0.021** (65/66 exact; 2-dp rounding of the mean) | **0.0033** (66/66 exact at 4 dp) |
| [T-59, T+1) | 0.22 | 0.30 | 0.33 |
| [T-61, T-1) | 0.26 | 0.36 | 0.36 |
| strike vs our [open-60, open) average | 0.0006 | 0.021 | 0.0033 |

- Window boundaries, index ids and the ≥ tie rule are implemented correctly; 198/198 results consistent with `avg ≥ strike`. 3 windows (18:30 UTC 10-27) had 0 ticks — the feed reconnect gap at 15:33 PT; the runner falls back to the replica then (1 SOL snapshot flagged).
- **p_cal during the settlement minute** uses observed samples + the distribution of the remaining ones: `proj = (1−r)·partial_twap + r·spot`, with `partial_twap` the running mean of received ticks in the window and `spot` the latest tick as the martingale forecast of each remaining sample; remaining-mean sd = `1.5·σ₁·r·√(r/3)` bp (Brownian mean over the remaining r minutes, 1.5× inflation). It does **not** substitute spot or a rolling average for the observed part. One imprecision: `r` is taken from the clock (`mins_left`) rather than `(60 − partial_n)/60`; at p50 cfb_age 0.5 s these differ by <1 sample. `scripts/p22_paired.py` uses the sample-count form.
- **Rounding/tie**: ETH settles on a 2-dp average; the tie (= YES) probability is ≈ P(|avg − K| < 0.005) ≈ 1–2% for a 1-bp final-minute sd (observed 1/66). p_cal treats P(avg ≥ K) as continuous, so for ETH NO positions it is overstated by up to ~1 pt at the margin. Not changed (P12 frozen; P13 threshold untouched) — logged as a pre-registered revision candidate.
- **Raw capture**: the feed stored parsed fields (`value`, `value_ts_ms`, `received_at_ms`, local `t`, `avg60`, `q15`) but not the verbatim message. Added `cfb_feed._raw_write` → `data/cfb/raw/<ASSET>.<UTCday>.raw.jsonl` `{t_local, raw}` (default on, `PREDHFT_CFB_RAW=0` disables; never read by the runner). Decision inputs (`spot`, `partial_twap`, `partial_n`) live in the trade/snapshot row and are never overwritten; `settle()` writes only `result`/`expiration_value`/`pnl`. Revised CF values can now be diffed against the exact message received.

## 5. Where the opportunity disappears; Thompson checks

Timestamp chain available in the data: CF publish (`value_ts_ms`) → local receipt (`cfb t`, +0.068 s p50) → forecast + book fetch (`books t`, `cfb_age_s` 0.51 s p50, 0.90 p90) → decision (`t_dec`) → re-check (`latency` 0.45 s, n=3 STALE rows; no submission exists in paper). Classification of every in-window snapshot that passed d/cap/depth/edge without the arm window (official era): **3 total** → 3 "would die in execution" (next snapshot ≤ 4 s: 2 `d_small`, 1 `edge_lt_min`), 0 survive; 2 of the 3 were also excluded by the arm window. "Gone before receipt" is unmeasurable without an exchange-side timestamp on book updates; the 0.07 s feed lag bounds it from below. **A touched quote in replay is not a fill**: marketable replay needs depth at the intended quantity and a latency assumption (used: top-3 depth, 1 s); resting replay needs queue position and adverse-selection assumptions (the `join` arms in `ts_levers` assume full queue priority and are therefore upper bounds).

Thompson (`ts_levers.py`), confirmed from source and tests: objective is **net cents per contract** (`100·(win − price − fee)`), not win rate; no-trade closes produce an empty dict and are skipped by `cluster_obs` (neither win nor loss); state persisted in `data/ts/levers_state.json`. Allocation history was only in `runner.log` → added `ts_levers.log_selection` → `data/ts/arm_selection.jsonl` (arm, window, cap, posterior n/mean/sd, P(best), selection probs) called at each sampling. Observation: most arms have n ≤ 1 with posterior mean −5c (prior shrink of one −52c loss); selection is prior-driven, which is correct behaviour at this n.
