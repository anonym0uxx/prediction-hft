## 1. Rules resolution (what settles these markets)

Read per ticker from `/markets/{ticker}` (`rules_primary`, `rules_secondary`, `strike_type`, `floor_strike`, `cap_strike`, `close_time`) and
from the series (`/series/KXHIGHTMIN`: settlement source "The Weather Company", contract terms GLOBALTEMPERATURE). Text of the
investigated market:

> If the maximum temperature recorded at Minneapolis (CLIMSP) for Oct 27, 2026, is less than 59° fahrenheit according to The Weather
> Company, then the market resolves to Yes. … the official and final value used to determine this market is the maximum/minimum
> temperature as reported by the Weather Company. … Preliminary Weather Company data may be subject to rounding and conversion
> differences from the final reported value. … If … a material error in the initial non-preliminary publication, Expiration may be held
> until … a non-materially-erroneous data revision or until the Expiration Date … If no data is available … resolve to the last fair price.

What The Weather Company publishes for Kalshi (weather.com/kalshi, "Daily Climate" tab, read 2026-10-28 12:20 PT): *"Daily values are
official Fahrenheit climate-report figures (the value Kalshi settles on)"* — i.e. the NWS CLI daily max/min for the station, republished
verbatim, with status Preliminary/Final. The hourly table on the same site is METAR-derived and explicitly *not* the settlement value.

Consequences encoded in `weather_lock.resolve_rules()`:

| item | resolution | source |
|---|---|---|
| station | CLI location code in rules text (`(CLIXXX)`); METAR station `K`+code (NYC → KNYC) | rules_primary |
| observation period | NWS climate day = **local standard time** midnight-to-midnight of the rules date; never DST. During DST the day runs 01:00–01:00 local clock. Kalshi's `close_time` (e.g. 06:00Z for Minneapolis Oct 27 = 00:00 CST) coincides with the window end — a consistency check that passes for every market seen. | NWS CLI convention; verified against close_time |
| cross-day window | none for daily high/low (the window is one LST day); the post-midnight DST hour (00:00–01:00 CDT of the *next* calendar day) belongs to the market's day | derived |
| precision | whole °F (CLI/TWC daily figures are integers); METAR tenths-°C converted → rounding slack 1 °F | CLI format |
| source hierarchy | TWC (settlement) > NWS CLI (what TWC republishes) > METAR hourly obs (supporting only) | series terms |
| strike semantics | `greater K`: YES iff value ≥ K+1; `less K`: YES iff value ≤ K−1; `between [floor, cap]`: inclusive integers (e.g. B68.5 = 68 ≤ v ≤ 69) | API strike fields + yes_sub_title |
| flags → not tradable | rules date ≠ ticker date; source not TWC/NWS; unit ≠ fahrenheit; unknown station tz; malformed strike | resolve_rules.flags |

## 2. Observation-day filtering (replaces the obs-count guard)

`metar_obs()` reads aviationweather JSON (`obsTime` epoch per report, T-group tenths preferred) and `filter_obs()` keeps only
`window_start ≤ obsTime < window_end` (and `≤ now`). No day arithmetic on the DDHHMMZ group, no counting heuristics. The guard
`metar n ≤ local_hour+2` is gone. CLI products are matched by their own `CLIMATE SUMMARY FOR <date>` line (prelim: `VALID [TODAY] AS OF`;
the 4:30 PM product says `VALID AS OF`, which the v1 parser mis-classified as final and therefore ignored — fixed). Each scoreboard row
now carries `window_start`, `window_end`, `metar.first_ts`, `metar.last_ts`, `metar.n`, `metar.max2/min2` so attribution is auditable.

## 3. Lock table (what is mathematically established regardless of later temperatures)

`value` = established running extreme (§3.1), `slack` = precision slack (0 for corroborated CLI, 1 °F for METAR-only).

| kind | strike_type | locks | never |
|---|---|---|---|
| high | greater K | value − slack ≥ K+1 → **YES** | NO (max can still rise) |
| high | less K | value − slack ≥ K → **NO** | YES intraday |
| high | between [lo,hi] | value − slack > hi → **NO** | YES (running max inside bucket establishes nothing) |
| low | greater K | value + slack ≤ K → **NO** | YES intraday |
| low | less K | value + slack ≤ K−1 → **YES** | NO (min can still fall) |
| low | between [lo,hi] | value + slack < lo → **NO** | YES |

Everything else is scored-only (probability recorded, never traded). Tests: `tests/test_weather_lock.py::test_lock_table`,
`::test_running_extreme_never_locks_yes_for_bucket_or_wrong_direction`.

### 3.1 Which value may lock (`established_value`)

| sources present | rule | tag |
|---|---|---|
| CLI and METAR, CLI more extreme than METAR by > `disagree_tol` (3 °F) | **no lock**, row basis `source_disagree`, p = null | source_disagree |
| CLI and METAR, CLI more extreme by 0..3 °F | CLI figure, slack 0 (TWC republishes the CLI figure) | cli+metar |
| CLI and METAR, METAR more extreme (CLI product stale) | METAR path below | metar |
| METAR only | needs a 2nd observation within 3 °F of the extreme (single-reading spikes do not lock); slack 1 | metar / metar_spike / metar_single_obs |
| CLI only (no METAR in window) | **no lock** (uncorroborated climate figure = the Minneapolis failure mode) | cli_only |

## 4. Exposure grouping

Group key `station_day = CLI:YYYY-MM-DD` (e.g. `MSP:2026-10-27`). Every open paper position whose settlement depends on the same
station-day — both series (high and low), any strike, either side — shares one cap: `station_day_cap = 12 $` of open cost
(`weather_lock.exposure_by_group`, netted per ticker so a YES and a NO on the same market do not double count). Global `risk.py`
caps remain unchanged (lot ≤ 20, 10 $/market, 30 $/event-date, settlement-timestamp caps, daily/weekly halts, error kill switch).

## 5. Population monitor

`first_lock_v2.json` stores the first locked call per ticker for the entire scoreboard population (traded or not): p, lock source,
kind, strike_type, station, value. From `window_end + 2 h` the runner polls `/markets/{ticker}`; a result that contradicts the call
→ `suspensions.json` gets the logic cell `lock_src|kind|strike_type` **and** the station; the runner refuses to trade any market that
hits either entry (rows still scored, `suspended` field set); an alert file lands in `data/weather-j1/outbox/`. A market still
`closed` an hour past `expected_expiration_time` without a result raises one `held_market` alert (Kalshi data-error hold). Clearing a
suspension is a manual edit of the file. Tests: `::test_population_monitor_suspends_logic_cell_and_station`,
`::test_runner_monitor_writes_suspension_alert_and_results`.

## 6. Forward fills (the only earnings definition)

Signal = scoreboard scan row (`signal_recv_t` = time the first orderbook response arrived). If locked, not flagged, not suspended, gap
≥ margin and room under the station-day cap: the book is fetched **again** (`book_recv_t`); the fill is at the re-checked offer + 1 ¢
tick (YES: best YES ask; NO: 1 − best YES bid), quantity ≤ re-checked displayed size, ≤ Kelly/lot/caps; fee `kalshi_taker_fee` (0.07·q·p·(1−p),
ceil to cent). The raw offer (`raw_offer_price`, `raw_offer_size`) and the signal-time offer (`signal_offer_*`) are logged separately
from the fill price; `simulate_fill` raises if `book_recv_t < signal_recv_t`. Nothing from the signal book sets the price. No fill is
booked when the re-checked gap after the tick is below margin or the displayed size is < 5.

## 7. Operating-cost measurement

`opcost.jsonl`, one row per cycle: `dur_s`, `poll_s`, API calls (`kalshi`, `nws` = api.weather.gov, `awc` = aviationweather),
`feed_errors`, `rows`, `markets`, `fills`. Live single-cycle measurement 2026-10-28 12:40 PT (48 series, 288 markets in window):
350 s per cycle, 216 Kalshi + 120 api.weather.gov + 24 aviationweather calls = 360 calls ≈ 62 calls/min while a cycle runs
(api.weather.gov product texts are cached across cycles, so steady state is ≈ 24+Δ NWS calls), 0 feed errors in that cycle (the v1
log shows api.weather.gov read timeouts are common; they are counted). With `--poll 120` the loop is cycle-bound (~6 min), the same
cadence v1 achieved (consecutive scoreboard rows for one ticker were ~22 min apart under v1). The daily report prints calls/min, cycle
duration, error rate and rows (§10).

## 8. Minneapolis KXHIGHTMIN-26OCT27-T59 — finding

Facts (all fetched 2026-10-28):

* Market: "Will the maximum temperature be <59° on Oct 27, 2026?" strike_type `less`, cap 59, YES = "58° or below", station CLIMSP,
  source The Weather Company, °F. Status at 12:13 PT: `closed`, no result, `expected_expiration_time` 2026-10-28T19:00Z passed;
  every sibling event (KXLOWTMIN-26OCT27, KXHIGHCHI-26OCT27, …) finalized at 19:00Z. **KXHIGHTMIN-26OCT27 is being held.** Last price 0.91;
  the book carried YES 0.96–0.98 from ~14:00 to ~22:00 PT on Oct 27 (our rows), open interest 7,242.
* NWS CLIMSP for Oct 27 (preliminary 4:32 PM CDT, preliminary 8:30 PM CDT, final 1:34 AM CDT Oct 28, all identical):
  **MAXIMUM 61 at 3:41 AM LST** (= 4:41 AM CDT), MINIMUM 44 at 4:54 AM.
* TWC weather.com/kalshi Daily Climate, Oct 27 2026, Minneapolis MSP: **Max 61, Min 44, status Final** (identical to the CLI, as the
  site states). TWC hourly mean for the day 49.8 °F.
* KMSP METARs for the whole CST day (06:00Z Oct 27 – 06:00Z Oct 28, IEM archive, hourly + specials): maximum **13.3 °C = 56 °F**
  (20:12Z); at 09:02Z and 09:24Z the temperature group is **missing** (sensor outage around 3–4 AM CST); 08:53Z = 7 °C, 09:53Z = 7 °C.
  1-minute ASOS (IEM asos1min, sparse) 08:30–10:30Z: all 44–45 °F, 09:40Z = 45 °F. No observation of any kind supports a 61.
* Our rows: `locked_prelim_cli` NO from 18:38 PT (8:30 PM product, prelim flag) with cli_max 61, metar max 56; the 4:32 PM product
  (cli_max 61 already visible from 14:44 PT) was ignored by the v1 prelim parser. Side NO, station MSP, date Oct 27 (settlement day =
  our local date; no date confusion), °F, timing: market was 0.96 YES before and after our lock.

Reading: the 61 is an ASOS 5-minute artifact during the 09:00–09:30Z sensor failure that propagated into the CLI and from there,
unchanged, into TWC's "Final" figure. The market priced the hourly record (56 → YES). Our call was not based on the weather but on the
published climate figure; the published figure is what the contract says settles the market — **unless** Kalshi invokes the
material-error clause, which the hold suggests it is considering. Three outcomes remain possible: settle on 61 (NO, our lock right,
market wrong at 0.96), settle on a revised CLI (YES, our lock wrong), or no revision by 2026-11-03 → "last fair price" (≈0.91–0.98 YES,
our lock wrong). The outcome is not knowable from the data; the position is not counted anywhere.

What changes because of it: a CLI figure more extreme than the METAR record by more than 3 °F no longer locks (`source_disagree`, §3.1);
in the replay this removes both MSP locks (T59 and B59.5) and no other. When the event settles, the population monitor compares the v1
call (recorded in first_lock.json / results) by hand — the v2 monitor only sees calls made after it started; if the result is YES the
cell `cli+metar|high|less` would not be suspended because v2 never locked it, but the finding is recorded here and in `wrong OLD` of
the replay file.

## 12. Not verified / open

* Kalshi's final disposition of KXHIGHTMIN-26OCT27 (held at time of writing).
* `disagree_tol = 3 °F` and the second-observation rule are judgment calls calibrated on 1.8 days of data (they remove exactly the
  disputed and the single-obs locks and nothing else in the replay); they have not been tested on a season of data.

  only way to find out; the expected fill count is low.
